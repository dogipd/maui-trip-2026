Understanding Hawaiian Canoe Crops

Ancient voyagers brought living "canoe plants" across the Pacific that formed the bedrock of Hawaiian culinary culture:

  • Kalo (Taro): The sacred root of Hawaii. The corm is steamed and mashed with water to make poi; young leaves are braised in coconut cream as lūʻau.
  • ʻUlu (Breadfruit): A large, starchy tree fruit that tastes like freshly baked potatoes or bread. Look for warm roasted ʻulu or crunchy ʻulu chips at roadside stands in Keʻanae (Day 5) and Olowalu (Day 3)!
  • ʻUala (Sweet Potato): Vivid purple-fleshed tuber that brings natural sweetness to luau plates and local spreads.
